For parents

Here are the things we value to make sure you and your child feel comfortable from day one.

The same tutor, every time

There are no handovers or substitute tutors. I get to know your child’s strengths, challenges, learning style, and school situation over time.

A short report after every lesson

After each lesson, I send a short LINE message/E-mail covering what we worked on, how things went, and what to focus on before the next lesson.

Regular check-ins

At key points, such as the end of each term, we take time to review your child’s progress, discuss what comes next, and talk about any goals or concerns.

Questions welcome between lessons

Homework worries, something that came up at school, or a schedule change before an exam — feel free to message me anytime.

No long-term commitment

There is no minimum commitment. You can pause or stop lessons at any time, making it easy to start without a long-term commitment.

Simple, clear pricing

You only pay for the lessons you take. There is no enrolment fee, cancellation fee, or monthly membership charge. Transportation fees apply to in-person lessons.

Our Philosophy

Carve Your Own Path.

Scroll to explore

01 /A World of Possibilities

I believe one of the greatest gifts parents hope to give their children through international education is a world of opportunities and possibilities.

02 /Defining Success on Your Own Terms

Yet much of today’s educational support seems to treat admission to prestigious universities as the ultimate goal, blindly prioritizing academic achievement in pursuit of society’s definition of “success.”

I was no different. Until I started to question for myself what success meant for me.

03 /Turning Aspirations into Drive

Academic effort is, of course, important. But I believe the starting point should be students’ own aspirations and interests. When they discover what they truly want to pursue, motivation to learn follows naturally.

I learned this firsthand. Reflecting on what truly mattered to me brought me back to tutoring, something I had continued since high school.

Seeing students light up when something clicks, and receiving updates from their families even after graduation, give me a sense of fulfillment and energy I find nowhere else.

04 /A Partner Along the Way

That realization inspired me to found Camino — Spanish for “path” — to help students discover their own aspirations and carve their own paths.

As a trusted partner to students and their families, my role is to be both a mentor who nurtures their curiosity and confidence, and a tutor who equips them with the academic foundation to turn those aspirations into reality.

01LESSON DESIGN

Lessons Designed by an International School Graduate

Essays, discussions, presentations, and more. Lessons reflect how international schools teach and assess, addressing today’s challenges and the skills students will need next.

Read moreShow less

At international schools, students need more than English proficiency and subject knowledge — they need to develop their own ideas and opinions—and learn to express them through essays, discussions, presentations, and more.

Traditional Japanese cram schools (juku) are generally designed around the Japanese school curriculum, so they may not fully address the learning styles and assessment criteria used by international schools. Drawing on the skills and standards valued by international schools, I design lessons around not only each student's current challenges, but the skills they will need as they move forward.

02FULLY PERSONALIZED

Fully Personalized

The subject, pace, and learning style all adapt to the student. Lessons are built around individual needs, with flexible scheduling wherever possible.

Read moreShow less

Because I teach one-on-one, every lesson can be tailored to the student's individual needs. From lesson style to scheduling, I adapt flexibly to each student's needs.

  • “Explain at my child’s English level, using some Japanese where it helps.”
  • “Don’t cram — move at a slower pace and check understanding as you go.”
  • “Take a short break roughly every 30 minutes.”
  • “Usually math, but prioritise essays whenever one is due.”

I adapt everything from the learning style to the subject itself. I also accommodate scheduling needs wherever possible, such as extra sessions before exams or a morning lesson on a school make-up holiday.

03REAL-WORLD GUIDANCE

Real-World Guidance from Professional Experience

Connect today’s learning to tomorrow’s possibilities. From universities and graduate schools in Japan and abroad to future careers, explore a wider range of options.

Read moreShow less

When you focus only on the work in front of you, it's easy to lose sight of why you're working so hard right now. That's why it's important to understand your options and connect what you're learning today to where you want to go tomorrow.

Drawing on my experience as an international school graduate, as well as my own path through higher education and work, I support students with an eye on expanding their future options — from universities and graduate schools in Japan and abroad to their future careers.

04BILINGUAL SUPPORT

Japanese–English Bilingual Support

In Japanese, in English, or a mix of both. Lessons adapt to each student’s understanding and English proficiency, supporting a gradual move toward learning in English.

Read moreShow less

Lessons are available in either Japanese or English. I switch flexibly between the two to match each student's level of understanding and English proficiency — leaning on Japanese while English is still difficult, gradually shifting toward learning in English as their confidence grows.

Having learned in both languages myself since childhood, I also advise on how to use each language appropriately and develop the confidence to use both languages comfortably and effectively.

05THINKING SKILLS

Building Stronger Thinking Skills

Start with a “why?”, organize ideas, and work toward a solution. A tutor with a global consulting background makes structured thinking accessible, sharing tools students can use for life.

Read moreShow less

Problem-solving isn't just a school skill—it's something we use throughout life. At international schools, students are encouraged to ask their own questions, look at things from multiple angles, and work toward solutions. I believe this is one reason so many international school graduates go on to thrive across a wide range of fields. It's also a skill that can be surprisingly difficult to develop.

That's why my lessons are built around curiosity and structured thinking. Students' own interests and "why?" questions become a natural source of motivation — and exploring those questions requires students to organize their thoughts and reason through problems step by step. This isn't an innate talent—it's a skill that can be developed. Unlike most private tutors, I also bring a background in global management consulting, and I translate those problem-solving methods into approaches that students can actually understand and apply — so they gain not just knowledge, but thinking tools they can carry with them for life. That's what makes Camino different.

About the tutor

Hiroki Takashima

From elementary through high school, I studied at Canadian Academy, an international school in Kobe, then went on to Waseda University and UCL (University College London), where I studied urban planning. After about seven years of consulting at McKinsey, Roland Berger, and PwC, I founded Camino.

My journey so far
Where it began

Discovering international school

While attending a Japanese kindergarten, I had the opportunity to join a summer program at an international school. Drawn to its new learning environment and perspectives, I later enrolled at Canadian Academy in Kobe. That experience sparked my curiosity about the wider world.

Elementary to high school

Studying the IB

I spent my entire elementary and secondary education at an international school. Through the International Baccalaureate (IB) Diploma Programme, I studied Math, Physics, Economics, and Japanese at Higher Level, and Language and Literature and Chemistry at Standard Level. Being surrounded by people from different cultures and perspectives helped me develop a broader view of the world.

University

Waseda University → UCL, London

I studied in the International Course at Waseda University’s Faculty of Science and Engineering before moving to London to study Urban Planning at UCL. For my undergraduate dissertation, I worked with a department responsible for urban development in Austria on the theme of “How can we create cities that are easier and more comfortable for women to navigate on foot?” During this time, I also interned at an architecture firm in London, where I received support for my research. Through these experiences, I gradually became more interested not only in designing cities, but in solving the broader challenges that cities face. I wanted to approach problems from multiple perspectives rather than through design alone, which ultimately led me to pursue a career in consulting.

A journey

Travelling the world

I have travelled as a backpacker through Europe, South America, and beyond, making the most of breaks between my studies and careers. I still enjoy visiting new places, exploring cities around the world, and catching up with friends I met during my student years.

Career

Consulting

I went on to work at international strategy consulting firms, working on projects ranging from government initiatives in smart cities to private-sector projects in autonomous driving and mobility. My work also brought me back to my long-standing interest in cities and urban development. Over the course of around seven years, I worked across both European and American consulting firms.

Now — Founding Camino

Consulting × education, a new kind of learning

I eventually returned to an ambition I had once considered earlier in my life: becoming a teacher. I obtained a US teaching certification and decided to bring together my experience in consulting and my passion for education. Through consulting, I developed the ability to think logically, ask the right questions, and work toward solutions to complex problems. I want to pass these skills on to students—not simply to help them succeed academically, but to give them the confidence and ability to carve their own path. That is why I named the business Camino, meaning “journey” or “path” in Spanish.
